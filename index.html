About me

Operating envelopes as a common language between network operators and grid users.

I am a doctoral researcher at the Smart Grids Lab of the University of Liège, supervised by Prof. Damien Ernst. My PhD, started in 2026, is about operating electrical networks through secure envelopes. My work sits between optimisation, regulatory analysis and distribution network management.

An operating envelope tells a grid user, period by period, how much power it may inject or draw without endangering the network. I study how these limits should be computed, how they should be allocated between users when the network is constrained, and how users could trade them among themselves.

I hold an MSc in Energy Engineering from the University of Liège. My master's thesis, Coordinating Transmission and Distribution System Operators Through Secure Operating Envelopes, was defended in June 2026. It computes power operating envelopes at the interfaces between transmission and distribution networks with a quadratic convex approximation of the AC power flow equations, using phase-shifter set-points and generator re-dispatch as control actions. It was carried out with RTE and Gérédis, and received the Elia Group Award and the AIM Award for the best master's thesis.

Alongside this, I have co-authored work on Remote Renewable Energy Hubs and on mobile battery storage for congested grids, and I am the main teaching assistant for the course ELEC0018-1 Energy markets and regulation.

Research

Three questions about the limits that networks place on their users.

Optimisation

Computing envelopes

Envelopes that remain safe under the AC power flow equations, obtained from convex relaxations such as the QC relaxation, including networks with phase-shifting transformers. With RTE and Gérédis.

Regulation

From contract to operation

How a long-term flexible connection contract becomes a limit applied close to real time, and how curtailment is shared between users of a constrained network. Includes analysis of European and Walloon law.

Market design

Markets for envelope rights

Secondary markets in which grid users exchange the capacity their envelopes grant them, so that scarce network room goes to those who value it most.
